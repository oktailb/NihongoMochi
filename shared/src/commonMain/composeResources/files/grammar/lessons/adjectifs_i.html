I-Adjectives (い形容詞)

In Japanese, there are two types of adjectives: I-adjectives and Na-adjectives. This lesson focuses on I-adjectives.

1. Characteristics

I-adjectives always end in the Hiragana syllable い (i). Unlike adjectives in English, they function similarly to verbs and can be conjugated directly to show tense.

Common Examples:

  • 大きい (ookii) - Big
  • 小さい (chiisai) - Small
  • 高い (takai) - Expensive / Tall
  • 新しい (atarashii) - New
  • おいしい (oishii) - Delicious

2. Basic Conjugation

To conjugate an I-adjective, you usually modify the final い (i).

Form Rule Example (Takai - Expensive)
Present (Affirmative) Keep the final "i" 高い (Takai) - It is expensive.
Present (Negative) Replace "i" with くない (kunai) 高くない (Takakunai) - It is not expensive.
Past (Affirmative) Replace "i" with かった (katta) 高かった (Takakatta) - It was expensive.
Past (Negative) Replace "i" with くなかった (kunakatta) 高くなかった (Takakunakatta) - It was not expensive.

3. The Exception: いい (Ii - Good)

The adjective "good" (ii) is irregular. Its conjugation is based on its old form よい (yoi).

  • Present: いい (ii)
  • Negative: よくない (yokunai)
  • Past: よかった (yokatta)
  • Past Negative: よくなかった (yokunakatta)

4. Usage in Sentences

  • Before a noun:
    高い車 (Takai kuruma) - An expensive car.
  • At the end of a sentence:
    この車は高いです。(Kono kuruma wa takai desu.) - This car is expensive.

Note: We add "desu" at the end for politeness, but the adjective itself carries the tense.
