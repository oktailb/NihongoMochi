The Nai-form (ない形) - Informal Negative

The Nai-form is used for the negative present tense in casual Japanese. It is also the building block for several important grammar structures like "must" or "please don't".

1. How to Conjugate

Conjugation depends on the verb group. Pay close attention to Group 1!

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final "u" sound of the dictionary form to the corresponding "a" sound, then add ない.

  • 書く (kaku) → 書か + ない = 書かない (kakanai) - Not write
  • 飲む (nomu) → 飲ま + ない = 飲まない (nomanai) - Not drink
  • 話す (hanasu) → 話さ + ない = 話さない (hanasanai) - Not speak

⚠️ Special Rule: If the verb ends in a plain う (u), it changes to わ (wa), not 'a'.

  • 買う (kau) → 買わ + ない = 買わない (kawanai) - Not buy

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Simply drop the final る (ru) and add ない.

  • 食べる (taberu) → 食べない (tabenai) - Not eat
  • 見る (miru) → 見ない (minai) - Not see

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

Memorize these carefully (note the sound change for 'kuru'):

  • する (suru) → しない (shinai)
  • 来る (kuru) → 来ない (konai - pronounced ko-nai)
  • ある (aru - to exist/have) → ない (nai) (Completely irregular!)

2. Common Grammar Patterns

The Nai-form is required for these essential structures:

  1. Requests not to do something (~naide kudasai):
    忘れないでください。(Wasurenaide kudasai) - Please don't forget.
  2. Obligation (~nakereba narimasen): "Must do" (lit: If you don't do, it's no good).
    勉強しなければなりません。(Benkyou shinakereba narimasen) - I must study.
  3. Doing something without doing another (~zuni / ~naide):
    寝ないで勉強しました。(Nenaide benkyou shimashita) - I studied without sleeping.
