The Potential Form (可能形) - "Can do"

The Potential Form is used to express that someone has the ability to do something, or that a situation allows for an action to happen.

1. How to Conjugate

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final "u" sound to the "e" sound and add る (ru).

  • 行く (iku - go) → 行け + る = 行ける (ikeru) - Can go
  • 話す (hanasu - speak) → 話せ + る = 話せる (hanaseru) - Can speak
  • 飲む (nomu - drink) → 飲め + る = 飲める (nomeru) - Can drink

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Replace the final る (ru) with られる (rareru).

  • 食べる (taberu) → 食べられる (taberareru) - Can eat
  • 見る (miru) → 見られる (mirareru) - Can see

Note: In casual speech, the "ra" is often dropped: 食べれる (tabereru).

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する (suru) → できる (dekiru) - Can do (Completely different!)
  • 来る (kuru) → 来られる (korareru) - Can come

2. Important Particle Change

When using the potential form, the object particle を (wo) often changes to が (ga).

  • 日本語を話します。(I speak Japanese.)
  • 日本語が話せます。(I can speak Japanese.)

3. "Potential Form" vs "~koto ga dekiru"

Both mean "can do", but the potential form is more common in daily conversation, while koto ga dekiru is slightly more formal or used for long verb phrases.
