Dictionary Form (辞書形)

The Dictionary Form is the most basic form of a Japanese verb. It is the form you will find when looking up words in a dictionary. It is also used for casual speech among friends and family.

1. The Three Verb Groups

Every Japanese verb belongs to one of three groups. Knowing the group is vital for any further conjugation.

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

These verbs always end in an "u" sound in their dictionary form.

  • 買う (kau) - to buy
  • 待つ (matsu) - to wait
  • 行く (iku) - to go
  • 飲む (nomu) - to drink
  • 話す (hanasu) - to speak

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

These verbs always end in る (ru), preceded by an "i" or "e" sound.

  • 食べる (taberu) - to eat
  • 見る (miru) - to see/watch
  • 寝る (neru) - to sleep

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

There are only two main irregular verbs in Japanese:

  • する (suru) - to do
  • 来る (kuru) - to come

2. Casual Usage

In casual Japanese, the dictionary form is used to express the Present Affirmative or the Future.

  • 明日、日本に行く。 (Ashita, nihon ni iku.) - Tomorrow, I will go to Japan.
  • 毎日、水を飲む。 (Mainichi, mizu wo nomu.) - Every day, I drink water.

3. Grammar Structures

The dictionary form is required for many common grammar patterns:

  • ...ことができる (...koto ga dekiru) - Can do something.
  • ...つもりです (...tsumori desu) - Plan/Intend to do something.
  • ...前に (...mae ni) - Before doing something.
