Tindakan & Status yang Sedang Berlangsung: ~Te Iru (~ている)

The ~te iru structure is one of the most frequently used patterns in Japanese. Depending on the verb, it describes either an action currently in progress or a lasting state resulting from a past action.

1. Cara Membentuknya

Take the Bentuk-te of any verb and add the verb いる (iru - to exist/be).

  • 読む (yomu) → 読んで (yonde) → 読んでいる (yonde iru) - Reading
  • 食べる (taberu) → 食べて (tabete) → 食べている (tabete iru) - Eating

2. Dua Makna Utama

A. Tindakan yang Sedang Berlangsung (Berkelanjutan)

With "action verbs" like eating, reading, or running, it means you are doing it sekarang.

  • 今、本を読んでいます。(Ima, hon wo yonde imasu) - I am reading a book now.

B. Keadaan Resultan

With "change-of-state verbs" like getting married, waking up, or sitting down, it describes a keadaan yang berlanjut after the action happened.

  • 結婚しています。(Kekkon shite imasu) - I am married (lit: I have married and am in that state).
  • 知っています。(Shitte imasu) - I know (lit: I have learned and now possess the knowledge).
  • 窓が閉まっています。(Mado ga shimatte imasu) - The window is closed.

3. Santai vs. Sopan

You can conjugate iru to match the politeness level you need:

  • Kasual: 食べている (tabete iru)
  • Sopan: 食べています (tabete imasu)
  • Masa Lalu Santai: 食べていた (tabete ita)
  • Masa Lalu yang Sopan: 食べていました (tabete imashita)

4. Menjatuhkan 'i' dengan santai

In casual spoken Japanese, the い (saya) is often dropped.

  • 何してるの？ (Nani shiteru no?) - What are you doing? (instead of sialan iru)
