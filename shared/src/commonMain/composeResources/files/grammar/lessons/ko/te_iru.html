진행 중인 작업 및 상태: ~Te Iru(~ていRU)

The ~테 이루 structure is one of the most frequently used patterns in Japanese. Depending on the verb, it describes either an action currently in progress or a lasting state resulting from a past action.

1. 작성방법

Take the 테폼 of any verb and add the verb 있어요 (iru - to exist/be).

  • 読む (yomu) → 読んで (yonde) → 대답해요 (yonde iru) - Reading
  • 食べる (taberu) → 食べて (tabete) → 식사하다 (tabete iru) - Eating

2. 두 가지 주요 의미

A. 진행 중인 작업(계속)

With "action verbs" like eating, reading, or running, it means you are doing it 지금 바로.

  • 今、本を대답해요。(Ima, hon wo yonde imasu) - I am reading a book now.

B. 결과 상태

With "change-of-state verbs" like getting married, waking up, or sitting down, it describes a 계속되는 상태 after the action happened.

  • 結婚그러게요。(Kekkon shite imasu) - I am married (lit: I have married and am in that state).
  • 知네네마스。(Shitte imasu) - I know (lit: I have learned and now possess the knowledge).
  • 窓が閉마네마스。(Mado ga shimatte imasu) - The window is closed.

3. 캐주얼 vs. 정중함

You can conjugate 이루 to match the politeness level you need:

  • 평상복: 食べている (tabete iru)
  • 예의 바른: 食べて마스 (tabete imasu)
  • 캐주얼 과거: 食べていた (tabete ita)
  • 공손한 과거: 食べてました (tabete imashita)

4. 캐주얼한 'i' 드랍

In casual spoken Japanese, the い (i) is often dropped.

  • 何して루の？ (Nani shite루 no?) - What are you doing? (instead of 젠장 이루)
