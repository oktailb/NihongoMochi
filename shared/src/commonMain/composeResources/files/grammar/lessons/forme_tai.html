Expressing Desire: ~Tai (~たい)

The ~tai form is used to express what the speaker wants to do. It is only used for your own desires or when asking someone else directly what they want.

1. How to Conjugate

The ~tai ending attaches to the Verb Stem (the part before ~masu).

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final "u" to "i" and add たい.

  • 行く (iku) → 行き (iki) → 行きたい (ikitai) - Want to go
  • 飲む (nomu) → 飲み (nomi) → 飲みたい (nomitai) - Want to drink

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Remove the final る (ru) and add たい.

  • 食べる (taberu) → 食べたい (tabetai) - Want to eat
  • 見る (miru) → 見たい (mitai) - Want to see

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

  • する (suru) → したい (shitai) - Want to do
  • 来る (kuru) → 来たい (kitai) - Want to come

2. It behaves like an I-Adjective!

Once you add ~tai, the word conjugates exactly like an I-adjective.

  • Affirmative: 行きたいです (I want to go)
  • Negative: 行きたくないです (I don't want to go)
  • Past: 行きたかったです (I wanted to go)
  • Past Negative: 行きたくなかったです (I didn't want to go)

3. Particle Usage

With ~tai, you can use either を or が to mark the object, though が often emphasizes the object of desire.

  • 水を飲みたい。(Mizu wo nomitai) - I want to drink water.
  • 水が飲みたい。(Mizu ga nomitai) - I want to drink water (specifically).
