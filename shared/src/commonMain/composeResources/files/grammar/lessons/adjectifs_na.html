Na-Adjectives (な形容詞)

The second type of Japanese adjectives is the Na-adjective. They are called this because they require a な (na) to be placed between the adjective and the noun they modify.

1. Characteristics

Na-adjectives often describe states, feelings, or qualities. Unlike I-adjectives, they do not conjugate themselves; instead, we conjugate the copula (desu/da) that follows them.

Common Examples:

  • 静か (shizuka) - Quiet
  • 有名 (yuumei) - Famous
  • きれい (kirei) - Beautiful / Clean (Note: Ends in "i" but is a Na-adjective!)
  • 好き (suki) - Like / Fond of
  • 元気 (genki) - Healthy / Energetic

2. Basic Conjugation (Polite)

We use the same endings as we do for nouns.

Form Ending Example (Shizuka - Quiet)
Present (Affirmative) です (desu) 静かです (Shizuka desu) - It is quiet.
Present (Negative) ではありません (de wa arimasen) 静かではありません (Shizuka de wa arimasen) - It is not quiet.
Past (Affirmative) でした (deshita) 静かでした (Shizuka deshita) - It was quiet.
Past (Negative) ではありませんでした 静かではありませんでした - It was not quiet.

3. Usage in Sentences

  • Before a noun (The "Na" rule):
    You must add な between the adjective and the noun.
    静かな町 (Shizuka na machi) - A quiet town.
    有名な人 (Yuumei na hito) - A famous person.
  • At the end of a sentence:
    この町は静かです。(Kono machi wa shizuka desu.) - This town is quiet. (No "na" here!)

4. Warning: The "i" Traps

A few Na-adjectives end in "i", which makes them look like I-adjectives. Watch out for these:

  • きれい (kirei) - Beautiful
  • 嫌い (kirai) - Dislike
  • 有名 (yuumei) - Famous (ends in "ei" sound)
