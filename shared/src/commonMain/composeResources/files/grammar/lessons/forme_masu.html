The Masu-form (ます形) - Polite Japanese

The Masu-form is used in formal or polite situations. Using it ensures you sound respectful to teachers, strangers, or colleagues. It is often one of the first things students learn.

1. How to Conjugate

Like all conjugations, the rules depend on the verb group.

Group 1: U-Verbs (Godan)

Change the final "u" sound of the dictionary form to the corresponding "i" sound, then add ます.

  • 買う (kau) → 買い + ます = 買います (kaimasu)
  • 待つ (matsu) → 待ち + ます = 待ちます (machimasu)
  • 行く (iku) → 行き + ます = 行きます (ikimasu)
  • 飲む (nomu) → 飲み + ます = 飲みます (nomimasu)
  • 話す (hanasu) → 話し + ます = 話します (hanashimasu)

Group 2: Ru-Verbs (Ichidan)

Simply drop the final る (ru) and add ます.

  • 食べる (taberu) → 食べます (tabemasu)
  • 見る (miru) → 見ます (mimasu)
  • 寝る (neru) → 寝ます (nemasu)

Group 3: Irregular Verbs

Memorize these two:

  • する (suru) → します (shimasu)
  • 来る (kuru) → 来ます (kimasu)

2. Verb Tenses (Polite)

The beauty of the Masu-form is that once you have the "Masu-stem", you can easily change the tense by changing the ending.

Tense Ending Example (Tabemasu)
Present Affirmative ～ます (-masu) 食べます (I eat)
Present Negative ～ません (-masen) 食べません (I do not eat)
Past Affirmative ～ました (-mashita) 食べました (I ate)
Past Negative ～ませんでした 食べませんでした (I did not eat)

3. The "Stem" (V-base)

The part before "masu" (e.g., kaī, tabe, shi) is called the Verb Stem. It is used as a building block for many other grammar points like ~tai (wanting to do) or ~nagara (while doing).
