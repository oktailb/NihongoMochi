Nominalization (こと and の)

In Japanese, you can turn a verb or an entire sentence into a "noun" so that it can be used as a subject, object, or with other particles. This process is called nominalization.

1. Using こと (koto)

こと means "thing" (intangible). Adding it after a verb in the dictionary form turns it into "the act of doing...".

  • 食べる (taberu) - To eat
  • 食べること (taberu koto) - Eating / The act of eating

Common Patterns:

  • ...ことができる (...koto ga dekiru) - Can do something (lit: The act of doing is possible).
    日本語を話すことができます。(Nihongo wo hanasu koto ga dekiru) - I can speak Japanese.
  • 趣味は...ことです (Shumi wa... koto desu) - My hobby is...
    私の趣味は写真を撮ることです。(Watashi no shumi wa shashin wo toru koto desu) - My hobby is taking photos.

2. Using の (no)

の is more casual and versatile than こと. It is often used with verbs of perception (seeing, hearing) or with specific phrases.

  • 歌うのを聞きました。(Utau no wo kikimashita) - I heard [someone] singing.
  • 泳ぐのが好きです。(Oyogu no ga suki desu) - I like swimming.

3. Koto vs No: When to use which?

Use こと (koto) for... Use の (no) for...
Formal definitions Personal feelings (like/dislike)
Fixed grammar (koto ga aru, etc.) Perception verbs (see, hear, feel)
At the end of a sentence (desu) Waiting or stopping an action

4. Summary Example

日本に行くことにしました。(Nihon ni iku koto ni shimashita) - I decided to go to Japan (lit: Decided on the 'thing' of going to Japan).
