Basic Particles (助詞 - Joshi)

Particles are small markers that follow words to indicate their role in a sentence. They are the "glue" that holds Japanese sentences together.

1. The Topic Marker: は (wa)

The particle は (written as 'ha' but pronounced 'wa') identifies the topic of the sentence—what you are talking about.

  • 私は学生です。(Watashi wa gakusei desu) - As for me, I am a student.

2. The Subject Marker: が (ga)

While は marks the topic, が marks the specific subject that performs an action or possesses a quality. It often provides new information.

  • 猫が好きです。(Neko ga suki desu) - I like cats (lit: Cats are likable).
  • だれが来ましたか？ (Dare ga kimashita ka?) - Who came?

3. The Object Marker: を (wo)

The particle を (pronounced 'o') marks the direct object—the thing being acted upon by a verb.

  • リンゴを食べます。(Ringo wo tabemasu) - I eat an apple.

4. The Destination/Time Marker: に (ni)

This versatile particle indicates direction, specific time, or location (of existence).

  • 日本に行きます。(Nihon ni ikimasu) - I go to Japan.
  • 八時に起きます。(Hachiji ni okimasu) - I wake up at 8 o'clock.

5. The Location of Action: で (de)

Indicates where an action takes place, or the means/tool used to do something.

  • 学校で勉強します。(Gakkou de benkyou shimasu) - I study at school.
  • ペンで書きます。(Pen de kakimasu) - I write with a pen.

6. The Possession/Relation Marker: の (no)

Indicates possession or connects two nouns. It works like "'s" in English.

  • 私の本 (Watashi no hon) - My book.
  • 日本語の先生 (Nihongo no sensei) - Japanese teacher.

を (o) Direct Object (Object of verb)
